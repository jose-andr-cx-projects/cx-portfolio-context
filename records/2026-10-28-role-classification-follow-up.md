## Position to communicate
Leadership should not be equated solely with positional or line-management authority. Relevant forms include identifying opportunities, shaping direction, building alignment, coordinating cross-functional delivery, and developing reusable capabilities.

Distinguish:
1. **Classification assessment:** Is the work already performed at the requisite complexity, accountability, autonomy, impact and leadership level under the applicable Class 6 criteria?
2. **Development:** Are there particular leadership skills to strengthen? Such development may occur within a reclassified role where all essential criteria are satisfied; it should not become an unspecified prerequisite.

This is José's argument, **not** a determination that the Class 6 requirements are met. Any mandatory criteria must be checked against the actual position description and classification framework.

## Candidate leadership evidence to substantiate
- **Snap Send Solve integration:** cross-functional service-design and operational/technical delivery coordination.
- **CX SharePoint Refresh:** employee-centred design direction, stakeholder engagement and implementation coordination.
- **Business Context Architecture:** strategic identification of fragmented services and proposal for reusable organisational capability.
- **Customer Account Analytics / Journey Intelligence:** initiating new approaches to customer-journey insight and decision support.
- **Customer Communication Governance:** converting design and operational findings into reusable rules and QA approaches.

These are **candidate examples**, not independently validated performance evidence. Link specific decisions influenced, stakeholder testimony, outcomes and documented responsibilities before using them in a formal submission. Avoid claiming formal people-management responsibilities not held.

My approach has been to lead through influence — shaping direction, building alignment, connecting people and ideas, and influencing decisions through those who hold the authority to make them.

Looking across my work, I believe there are tangible examples of this. From leading the Snap Send Solve integration and CX SharePoint Refresh, to developing approaches around customer account analytics, communication governance and business context architecture, I've consistently taken initiative beyond delivering assigned tasks. Much of this work has involved identifying opportunities, bringing stakeholders together, establishing direction and helping translate ideas into practical outcomes.

I recognise there are areas where I can continue developing my leadership capability, and I'm open to that feedback. However, I would challenge the assumption that any remaining development necessarily needs to happen before progressing to Class 6.

My view is that the classification should primarily reflect the complexity, accountability, autonomy and contribution of the work I'm already performing. Where there are specific leadership development needs, these could be addressed within the role, provided the essential classification requirements are met.

I also wonder whether we're placing too much weight on visible or positional leadership, rather than recognising the influence and capability-building leadership that much of my work requires.

I'm not suggesting leadership development isn't important. Rather, I'd like us to distinguish between a genuine gap against the classification requirements and an opportunity for continued professional growth.

I appreciate you taking this forward and would welcome the opportunity to understand what specific evidence would help strengthen the case, particularly if leadership remains a concern.

Thanks again for your support.
